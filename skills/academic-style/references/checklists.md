# Checklists

Two passes. The first is for a draft you are still shaping; the second is the error sweep
before it leaves your hands.

## Draft assessment

**Title and keywords.** Does the title reflect the content and hint at the result? Are the
significant words near the beginning? Do the keywords avoid repeating title words, and would
the intended reader search for them?

**Abstract.** Does it give background, problem, methods, results, and a conclusion? Does it
contain numbers? Do its methods, results, and conclusions match the main text?

**Introduction.** Does it open broad and narrow? Does it place the study in what is known,
name the gap, and close with an explicit aim or testable hypothesis? Is it three or four
paragraphs?

**Materials and methods.** Were the design, sampling, and statistics appropriate? Could
someone repeat the work from this description? Is anything described whose results are never
reported? Are the software and the AI use disclosed?

**Results.** Do they answer the questions posed in the Introduction, in that order? Is every
float necessary, and is anything duplicated between text and float? Could any two floats be
combined, or any deleted? Can each float be understood without the text?

**Discussion.** Does it open by relating to the aims? Does it discuss rather than repeat? Are
similarities and differences with previous work explained? Are limitations given with the
likely direction of bias? Is speculation labelled as speculation? Does it close on the key
message?

**Technical.** Does the document conform to the target's specifications? Is the reference list
complete in both directions? Is the abstract within the word limit? Is the document within the
length limit?

## Common errors, by section

**Title** — too long and vague; does not describe the content; unfamiliar abbreviations or
jargon; witty at the expense of clear.

**Running head** — too long; does not match the content.

**Keywords** — repeat title words; abbreviations nobody would search for; terms unsuitable for
indexing.

**Abstract** — no background or aims; no experimental design; methods that differ from
Materials and methods; vague results, or results that differ from the main text; no concluding
statement, or one that differs from the document's own conclusion; over the word limit.

**Introduction** — background not concise; purpose not clear; importance and originality not
stated; unrelated material; material that belongs in the Discussion.

**Materials and methods** — design and sampling unclear; not reproducible from the
description; statistics described poorly or wrongly; methods whose results are never reported;
missing software or AI disclosure.

**Results** — incomplete or incorrect presentation; data from studies not described here;
results not relevant to the stated aims; text repeating the floats; material that belongs in
Materials and methods or the Discussion.

**Discussion** — biased, omitting others' findings; repeating rather than discussing; main
findings and their importance not highlighted; limitations not described; **speculation not
characterised as speculation**; unrelated information; misleading citation; overstated
importance; verbose, without logical flow.

**Conclusions** — repeats other sections; statements the findings do not support; not related
to the aims; speculative rather than well-founded; vague.

**Citations and references** — text and list do not match in both directions; incomplete or
incorrect publication details; inconsistent formatting; wrong style for the target.

## Deviations from author instructions

The five commonest reasons a manuscript is returned before review:

1. General organisation, including how the document is divided into sections.
2. Format of in-text citations and the reference list.
3. Presentation and numbering of figures and tables.
4. Style for formulae, units, and reporting of statistical tests.
5. Species names not in the required form.

Also frequent: missing running head, keywords, page numbers, or line numbers; wrong
affiliation format; wrong section numbering; headings in all capitals; footnotes and
appendices where the journal forbids them; abbreviations defined nowhere or defined twice.

## Before you hand it over

- Every float has a caption, is cited in the text, and is cited nowhere that does not exist.
- Every number in the prose comes from code, not from a keyboard.
- Every citation resolves to a real source in the form written; no `[VERIFY]` markers remain
  unresolved.
- Units, symbols, terminology, and species names are identical everywhere they appear —
  prose, equations, tables, captions, legends, and code.
- No wording is left over from a method, variable, unit, or figure that has since changed.
- The AI disclosure names every model that worked on the document, and the software citations
  match the packages actually used.
- Spelling and grammar have been checked. Errors that could have been caught cost the reader's
  confidence in everything else.
